01Start the dealEstablish the account, origin, ownership and qualification needed to start a credible opportunity.+

Course overview

Learn how to turn an introduction into a traceable, owned and qualified opportunity. Follow the correct intake path, establish the customer problem and funding context, and separate the source of the deal from how it will transact.

What you’ll learn

  • Identify the real origin and sourcing reference, including partner registration scenarios.
  • Review SAL acceptance and apply CHAMPS at the correct inbound or seller-direct gate.
  • Confirm account tier, coverage, use case, route to market and the first qualified pipeline milestone.

What you’ll take away

An actionable handoff: correct account and source, a responsible seller, supported qualification and a clear next conversation.

When to use it

Use this stream when creating a deal, reviewing a handoff or preparing to enter Discovery.

Opportunity source and sourcing credit

Opportunity Source records the verified origin of a deal: Direct, BDR, Marketing or Partner. Its sourcing reference identifies the originating user, campaign or partner.

At a glance

  • Direct / BDR → Sourcing User; Marketing → Sourcing Campaign; Partner → Sourcing Partner.
  • Registration and origination are separate: either can exist without the other.
  • Accepted partner origin is protected; authorized corrections require evidence and an audit trail.

Scenario

Illustrative

A BDR identifies a research lab and hands it to an AE. The AE creates the record from the handoff. They retain BDR as the source and the originating BDR as Sourcing User. The new AE owns the deal; that assignment does not turn the source into Direct.

Full lesson
What you’ll be able to do

Identify the correct source and sourcing reference without confusing origin, ownership or fulfillment.

Source describes origin; Opportunity Owner describes accountability; Route to Market describes how the sale will transact.

How to do it

  1. Trace the introduction

    Review the originating handoff or registration and identify the source evidence before creation.

  2. Select the matching reference

    Link the actual originating person, campaign or partner. A background integration user is not the sourcing person.

  3. Resolve ambiguity

    If the handoff lacks its source reference, resolve it with the originating team before creating the opportunity. Do not select a convenient substitute.

  4. Preserve and correct carefully

    Keep accepted partner source when ownership or registration changes. Request an authorized RevOps / Partner Ops correction with evidence and a reason if the origin is wrong; a contribution override is a separate action.

Use the current Sourcing Campaign reference until the Marketing migration is confirmed.

Questions to use

“Who initiated this opportunity?”

“Which original handoff, campaign or registration supports that attribution?”

What good looks like

Too vague or unsupported

Direct, because the AE entered the record.

Specific and supported

BDR source; originating BDR linked; assigned AE owns the opportunity; handoff evidence retained.

Put it into practice

A seller originates a deal. A reseller later registers it and will transact it. What source and references should be recorded?

Compare your answer

Keep Direct with the originating seller as Sourcing User. Link the registration and associated reseller separately. A registration does not make the reseller the originating source.

Knowledge check

Can a verified partner-origin deal be Source = Partner without an approved registration?

Show answer and why

Yes. Record the verified Sourcing Partner and origin evidence. Registration is a separate relationship and does not determine origin.

Use this on your next deal

Origin → supporting handoff → matching sourcing reference. Record the current owner and transaction role separately.

MQL, AQL, SAL and SQL

MQL and AQL are distinct Contact qualification paths. Opportunity creation records SAL; first qualified pipeline entry records SQL, including valid skips past Discovery.

At a glance

  • Keep MQL and AQL paths distinct.
  • Record SAL at creation and SQL at first qualified pipeline entry, including valid skips.
  • Pipeline Date and entry Amount stamp once at first valid entry to Stages 1–8. Stage Dates record actual visits only.

Scenario

Illustrative

PAX.AI qualifies a prospect as AQL. An opportunity is created in Validate, recording SAL. After seller acceptance and CHAMPS qualification it enters Discovery, recording SQL. The team can explain the AQL-to-SAL-to-SQL path without inventing an MQL event.

Full lesson
What you’ll be able to do

Reconstruct the correct qualification milestones for an inbound or seller-direct opportunity.

SAL is a lifecycle milestone. Seller acceptance is a separate decision. MQL and AQL are not mandatory sequential steps.

How to do it

  1. Identify the path

    Establish whether qualification came from Marketing, PAX.AI, a BDR, a partner or the seller.

  2. Resolve the person

    Associate the designated originating Contact with the opportunity and correct account context. Do not update every committee member as a conversion.

  3. Follow the milestones

    For inbound intake, acceptance and all applicable qualification gates must pass before the first valid entry to Stages 1–8 records SQL. Valid skips count. Seller-self-qualified direct Stage 1 creation records SAL and SQL together under its documented path.

  4. Protect reporting

    Capture SQL, Pipeline Date and Pipeline Entry Amount at the same successful first pipeline entry. Stamp Stage 0–6 Dates only for actual visits; skipped stages stay blank and re-entry never resets first dates or duplicates SQL. Keep transition history for revisits. Do not fabricate MQL/AQL or downgrade existing customer lifecycle states.

PAX.AI is a qualification channel. Confirm its underlying Opportunity Source with RevOps rather than inventing a new source choice.

Questions to use

“Which Contact was qualified for this opportunity?”

“Was the opportunity inbound or genuinely seller-self-qualified?”

What good looks like

Too vague or unsupported

Every SQL must first be recorded as both MQL and AQL.

Specific and supported

AQL → SAL → SQL, with separate seller-acceptance evidence and one linked opportunity episode.

Put it into practice

An opportunity moves from Stage 0 directly to Stage 4 after all crossed gates pass. Which dates and amount should the system capture?

Compare your answer

Stamp SQL, Stage 4 Date and Pipeline Date at the same successful event, plus Pipeline Entry Amount and currency. Keep Stage 0 Date and leave never-visited Stage 1–3 Dates blank. All crossed gates must pass. Regression, retries and later pricing changes do not create another SQL or reset the pipeline snapshot.

Knowledge check

A deal validly skips from Stage 0 to Stage 4. Does SQL require a Stage 1 Date?

Show answer and why

No. Record SQL when the qualified transition succeeds, together with Pipeline Date and Stage 4 Date. Stage 1 Date remains blank because Discovery was not visited. Filling CHAMPS while staying in Stage 0 alone does not record SQL.

Use this on your next deal

For each episode: originating Contact + underlying source + qualification path + actual milestone. Keep separate opportunities reportable.

Review and accept a SAL

The assigned seller’s decision to accept or reject an opportunity handoff. This decision is separate from the SAL milestone recorded at opportunity creation.

At a glance

  • Review the original handoff and seller assignment.
  • Record the acceptance or rejection decision and supporting context.
  • For inbound intake, complete all five CHAMPS answers before Stage 1.

Scenario

Illustrative

An AE receives an attractive lab opportunity but the handoff has no funding information. The AE accepts responsibility, then works with the BDR to complete Money and the remaining CHAMPS evidence. They enter Discovery only after the inbound gate is complete.

Full lesson
What you’ll be able to do

Make a clear handoff decision and explain what still needs to be satisfied before Discovery.

Accepted does not itself mean qualified for Stage 1. Assignment and the inbound CHAMPS requirements must also be satisfied.

How to do it

  1. Read the handoff

    Confirm customer context, source, originating Contact and accountable seller.

  2. Assess the disposition

    Decide whether to accept the handoff. When rejecting, explain the specific gap so the originating team can learn or act.

  3. Complete qualification

    For inbound intake, review Challenge, Authority, Money, Priority and Stack / Storage. Acceptance does not substitute for these answers. When requesting the next stage, the proposed Update Stage flow presents missing evidence and retains existing values. Complete what you are authorized to supply; ask the named owner for protected technical or approval evidence. The stage advances only when all gates pass.

  4. Document the decision

    Use the supported acceptance/rejection process so actor and time are recorded. Marketing SAL rejection requires date and notes; acceptance requires its date.

Use the supported acceptance/rejection process. Do not assume SAL Start Date means acceptance or SAL Completion Date means rejection.

Questions to use

“What evidence makes this handoff actionable?”

“What is missing, and who can resolve it?”

What good looks like

Too vague or unsupported

Accepted; moved to Discovery although CHAMPS is incomplete.

Specific and supported

Accepted by the assigned AE; source and Contact verified; all five inbound CHAMPS answers supported before SQL.

Put it into practice

An inbound handoff has a Contact but no identifiable project or relevant need. Draft a rejection note that helps the originating team act.

Compare your answer

“Rejected: the conversation did not establish an active project or a relevant customer challenge. Re-engage to confirm the affected workflow and why a change is needed; provide that evidence for a new review.” This explains the gap without inventing a SQL or a loss decision.

Knowledge check

You accept an inbound opportunity, but its Money answer is missing. Can you move it into Discovery, and why?

Show answer and why

No. Acceptance establishes the seller’s disposition, not completion of qualification. Resolve all five inbound CHAMPS answers, plus the assignment and Contact requirements, before Stage 1. Use Update Stage to supply the missing evidence; cancelling or failing validation leaves the stage and entry dates unchanged.

Use this on your next deal

Decision + evidence + missing qualification + next action. Keep acceptance separate from the SAL creation milestone.

CHAMPS: Challenge

The customer’s initial problem or unmet need, supported by customer evidence.

At a glance

  • Ask about a specific problem, not a product request.
  • Record who experiences it and a recent example.
  • Inbound: before Stage 1. Seller-qualified direct entry: before Stage 2.

Scenario

Illustrative

A lab requests more storage. The BDR asks what triggered the request. The researcher explains that datasets wait overnight before processing. The handoff now describes delayed analysis rather than simply “needs more capacity.”

Full lesson
What you’ll be able to do

Write a customer-evidenced Challenge statement that identifies the problem and affected workflow.

Challenge captures the initial problem. Identify Pain later develops its impact and business consequences.

How to do it

  1. Start with the trigger

    Ask what happened that caused the customer to seek a change.

  2. Locate the impact

    Identify the team, workload and recurring interruption. Separate observation from assumption.

  3. Check understanding

    Restate the problem in the customer’s words and invite correction.

  4. Record evidence

    Capture the situation and source in CHAMPS Challenge. Carry the answer into deeper pain discovery without replacing it.

Questions to use

“What happened the last time this became a problem?”

“Who is affected and what do they have to do differently?”

What good looks like

Too vague or unsupported

Customer needs a faster product.

Specific and supported

Research team reports overnight data staging delays before each analysis run; lab manager confirmed the workflow.

Put it into practice

The customer says “our storage is old.” Ask two follow-ups, then draft a Challenge statement without inventing answers.

Compare your answer

Ask: “What happened recently that made the age a problem?” and “Who was affected, and what could they not do?” Until answered, record: “Customer reports aging storage; the operational problem and affected workflow still need validation.” Replace that uncertainty with the customer’s evidence after the discussion.

Knowledge check

The buyer requests twice the capacity but has not explained why. What should you discover before calling this a validated Challenge?

Show answer and why

Ask what activity is constrained today, who experiences the constraint and a recent example. A requested specification is evidence of a request, not yet of the underlying problem.

Use this on your next deal

“[Team] experiences [specific problem] during [workflow], as shown by [customer example].”

CHAMPS: Authority

The contact’s role and influence in the buying decision, including access to those who approve the purchase.

At a glance

  • Establish the contact’s role in the decision.
  • Ask who approves funding and who evaluates the solution.
  • Record evidence and remaining access gaps.

Scenario

Illustrative

A storage architect can recommend the design but says the infrastructure director owns the budget. The AE records both roles and asks the architect to arrange a joint discussion rather than describing the architect as the budget holder.

Full lesson
What you’ll be able to do

Distinguish a contact’s recommendation power from budget, technical and approval authority.

An influential evaluator is not automatically the Economic Buyer. Early authority discovery identifies the path to decision makers.

How to do it

  1. Understand involvement

    Ask how the contact participated in the last comparable purchase.

  2. Map the limits

    Separate recommendation, technical veto, budget authority and signature responsibilities.

  3. Find the access path

    Agree how to involve the relevant decision makers and record any uncertainty.

  4. Document the handoff

    Capture the known roles in Authority; refine the buying committee as discovery progresses. Complete inbound CHAMPS before Stage 1 or seller-direct CHAMPS before Stage 2.

Questions to use

“Who else needs to agree for this project to happen?”

“Who could approve or stop the spend?”

What good looks like

Too vague or unsupported

Senior title, so probably the decision maker.

Specific and supported

Architect owns technical recommendation; director controls project budget; introduction requested.

Put it into practice

A contact says “I decide everything,” but mentions a finance review. Write two questions and the provisional authority note.

Compare your answer

Ask: “What can you approve directly?” and “What decision does finance make, and can it stop the purchase?” Note: “Contact claims overall decision responsibility; finance’s approval role and any spending limit remain unconfirmed.” Do not resolve the ambiguity by title alone.

Knowledge check

The architect can veto the design but cannot release funds. How should that affect your buying map?

Show answer and why

Record the architect’s technical authority and identify who controls the budget separately. Strong technical influence does not prove Economic Buyer authority.

Use this on your next deal

Person → decision they control → approval limit or dependency → evidence → access still needed.

CHAMPS: Money

Evidence of the customer’s funding, funding access and ability to pay for the project.

At a glance

  • Ask how the project will be funded.
  • Separate known budget, estimates and approval conditions.
  • Document the source of the funding evidence.

Scenario

Illustrative

A customer estimates a $400,000 project but says approval depends on a business case. The seller records the estimate and its approval condition. They do not call the funding approved simply because a number was discussed.

Full lesson
What you’ll be able to do

Capture funding evidence while clearly distinguishing estimates, requests and approved budget.

A funding hypothesis is different from an approved budget. Record the status accurately rather than treating an estimate as approval.

How to do it

  1. Explore the mechanism

    Ask whether the project uses an allocated budget, a new request or another funding route.

  2. Establish the status

    Clarify which amounts are confirmed and which are estimates; identify the approving person.

  3. Identify the condition

    Learn what evidence is needed for funding and when the decision can occur.

  4. Record faithfully

    Capture the amount or commercial capacity evidence, funding status and unresolved conditions in Money. Apply the source-dependent CHAMPS timing.

Questions to use

“Is this funding allocated or still being requested?”

“What must be demonstrated for the funding to be approved?”

What good looks like

Too vague or unsupported

Budget: $400,000 approved.

Specific and supported

Customer estimate: $400,000; funding request depends on finance accepting the business case.

Put it into practice

The customer says, “We expect $400,000, but finance has not approved it.” Rewrite the Money answer and name the next question.

Compare your answer

“Customer estimates a $400,000 project budget; finance approval is pending.” Next ask what finance needs to approve the allocation and who is accountable for that decision. Keep the amount labeled as an estimate.

Knowledge check

A buyer names a budget range but cannot identify its funding route or approver. What can you responsibly record?

Show answer and why

Record the buyer-provided range and its source, with funding route and approval status still unconfirmed. Seek those facts instead of upgrading the estimate to an approved budget.

Use this on your next deal

Amount or range + funding route + approval status + approver + remaining condition. Label unverified information.

CHAMPS: Priority

The urgency and business importance of solving the customer’s problem, including the consequence of delay.

At a glance

  • Identify the customer’s event or deadline.
  • Explore the consequence of delay.
  • Confirm priority relative to competing projects.

Scenario

Illustrative

A lab wants the platform before a new research program starts in January. Missing that date would delay onboarding. The AE records the program milestone and consequence, then checks whether funding and installation can realistically support it.

Full lesson
What you’ll be able to do

Validate customer urgency through a business event, a consequence and competing priorities.

A seller’s quarter-end target is not evidence of customer urgency.

How to do it

  1. Find the event

    Ask why the customer is addressing the issue now and which business activity depends on it.

  2. Test the consequence

    Explore what happens if the project is delayed or no change is made.

  3. Compare priorities

    Ask what other initiatives compete for the same people, funds and approval time.

  4. Record a supported timeline

    Capture the event, impact and customer confirmation in Priority. Complete it at the appropriate inbound or seller-direct CHAMPS gate.

Questions to use

“What changes if this is not solved by that date?”

“Where does this sit among the team’s other funded priorities?”

What good looks like

Too vague or unsupported

Must close this quarter.

Specific and supported

January program launch requires the new workflow; director confirms a delay would postpone researcher onboarding.

Put it into practice

The prospect says “ASAP.” Write three questions that would make this useful for qualification.

Compare your answer

“Which event or deadline makes this urgent?” “What happens if it is delayed?” “Which other projects compete for the same funding or people?” Together these test timing, consequence and relative importance.

Knowledge check

The AE needs a quarter-end close, but the customer sees no consequence in waiting. What should the Priority answer say?

Show answer and why

Describe the customer’s actual priority and the absence of a confirmed deadline. The seller’s target does not supply customer urgency. Continue discovery rather than presenting quarter-end as a customer requirement.

Use this on your next deal

Customer event/date → consequence of delay → competing priorities → confirming person.

CHAMPS: Stack / Storage

The customer’s current technical environment, workloads and relevant storage requirements.

At a glance

  • Understand the current workload and environment.
  • Identify the storage constraint and dependencies.
  • Separate customer facts from items the SE still needs to validate.

Scenario

Illustrative

A lab uses shared storage for ingest and model training. The BDR captures capacity, workload patterns and a reported ingest bottleneck, then flags throughput validation for the SE. They avoid prescribing a configuration during intake.

Full lesson
What you’ll be able to do

Create a useful technical handoff that separates measured facts from estimates and validation gaps.

Early qualification identifies context; it does not commit to a final configuration or assert a Technical Win.

How to do it

  1. Map the workflow

    Follow the data from creation through storage, processing and access.

  2. Identify constraints

    Ask about capacity, performance, integration and migration concerns relevant to that workflow.

  3. Capture the evidence quality

    Distinguish measurements from customer estimates and secondhand claims.

  4. Hand off intelligently

    Record the environment in CHAMPS and the questions needing SE validation. Complete the appropriate qualification gate without presenting estimates as test results.

Questions to use

“Where does the workload slow down today?”

“Which applications, interfaces or migration dependencies must be supported?”

What good looks like

Too vague or unsupported

Needs high-performance storage.

Specific and supported

Shared storage supports ingest and training; reported ingest bottleneck needs validation with the customer workload.

Put it into practice

The lab measured 8-hour staging. It estimates a 30% data increase and suspects a network bottleneck. Write the handoff.

Compare your answer

“Measured: staging takes 8 hours under the observed conditions. Customer estimate: data volume may grow 30%. Hypothesis: network bottleneck. SE to validate workload conditions, growth assumptions and the actual limiting component.” Do not present the suspected bottleneck as a test result.

Knowledge check

The intake lists product names and capacity but no workflow. What should the SE still learn before designing?

Show answer and why

How data is ingested, stored, processed and accessed; where the constraint occurs; and relevant integration or migration dependencies. Product and capacity names alone do not establish technical fit.

Use this on your next deal

Workflow + environment + observed constraint + dependencies. Mark each item measured, reported or still to validate.

Discover the use case

The activity or workflow the customer intends to support with the solution, and the outcome they want to achieve.

At a glance

  • Name the customer workflow and its users.
  • Connect the workflow to the intended outcome.
  • Complete discovery and AE/Primary SE assignment before Stage 2.

Scenario

Illustrative

A prospect asks about storage capacity. Discovery reveals that the requirement supports a new AI training pipeline, with researchers waiting for data preparation. The seller records the use case and context so the SE can design for the actual workflow.

Full lesson
What you’ll be able to do

Translate requested specifications into a clear customer workflow and intended outcome.

A product family describes what is sold. A use case describes what the customer will do with it.

How to do it

  1. Walk the workflow

    Ask the customer to describe the activity from input to result and identify its users.

  2. Connect the outcome

    Explore what a successful change enables for those users and why that matters.

  3. Choose and explain

    Select the appropriate Use Case and add the specific workload, constraints and intended result in Additional Use Case Information.

  4. Coordinate discovery

    Confirm the assigned AE and Primary SE, complete CHAMPS at its required timing, and establish at least one Contact Role before Stage 2.

Questions to use

“What will your users be able to do after this change?”

“Which workload should our design be evaluated against?”

What good looks like

Too vague or unsupported

Use case: storage.

Specific and supported

AI model training; researchers need faster dataset preparation to reduce idle time between experiments.

Put it into practice

A request says “more capacity and throughput.” The customer explains that researchers wait for dataset preparation between model-training runs. Write the use case.

Compare your answer

“AI model training: researchers need datasets prepared faster between experiments so they spend less time waiting for the next run.” Capture the requested specifications as design inputs, not as the use case itself.

Knowledge check

Two customers request the same product, one for archiving and one for model training. Should their Use Case information be identical?

Show answer and why

No. Describe each customer’s activity, users and desired outcome. Similar products can serve different workflows with different evaluation requirements.

Use this on your next deal

“[Users] need to [activity] so they can [intended outcome], within [relevant constraints].”

Choose the route to market

How the sale will transact: Direct or Indirect. Indirect requires an applicable Indirect Route Type.

At a glance

  • Confirm how the customer expects to buy.
  • For Indirect, select the applicable Indirect Route Type.
  • Direct pricing: notify the manager and relevant partner coverage before release; existing approvals still apply.

Scenario

Illustrative

A Marketing-sourced customer chooses to purchase through a reseller. The AE keeps Marketing and the original campaign as sourcing attribution, sets Route to Market to Indirect, and selects the applicable indirect type.

Full lesson
What you’ll be able to do

Record the purchasing route without rewriting how the opportunity originated.

Transaction route does not rewrite the original Opportunity Source or establish partner-contribution credit.

How to do it

  1. Ask about purchasing

    Establish whether the customer buys from DDN or through another party.

  2. Validate participants

    Confirm the commercial role of the partner and involve the Partner Manager when needed.

  3. Record the route

    Complete Route to Market during discovery. Indirect requires its type; Direct does not require a second selection.

  4. Handle changes

    If the route changes to Direct, the old indirect type no longer drives current attribution; retain its history rather than rewriting origin. Notify the manager and relevant partner coverage before Direct customer pricing; capture the route earlier if requesting a Stage 0–1 budgetary quote. Notification itself is not a blanket approval gate.

Questions to use

“Which entity will the customer place the order with?”

“Has that purchasing route been confirmed by the customer?”

What good looks like

Too vague or unsupported

Changed source to Channel Partners because the customer will buy through a reseller.

Specific and supported

Marketing origin retained; Indirect route and its type reflect the confirmed reseller transaction.

Put it into practice

A BDR-origin opportunity will transact through a reseller. Identify the source, sourcing reference, route and additional route information.

Compare your answer

Keep BDR as Opportunity Source and the originating BDR as Sourcing User. Set Route to Market to Indirect and choose the applicable supported Indirect Route Type. Capture the reseller’s involvement separately.

Knowledge check

The customer switches from a reseller purchase to Direct. What happens to the source and the old indirect type?

Show answer and why

The original source remains. The old Indirect Route Type becomes inactive for current attribution and is hidden, with its history preserved. Direct does not need a second route selection.

Use this on your next deal

Origin answers “where did it come from?” Route answers “how will the customer buy?” Confirm each separately.

Account tier and coverage

Account Tier classifies an account as Tier 1, Tier 2 or Tier 3. Partner coverage and the partner’s admin contact live on the Account; RevOps Notes provides restricted operational context.

At a glance

  • Use the agreed Account Tier; do not guess a tier from deal size.
  • Keep partner manager, technical lead and Partner Admin Contact current.
  • RevOps owns RevOps Notes; a contact or team role does not grant approval rights.

Scenario

Illustrative

A partner account changes coverage. Partner Ops updates the DDN partner manager and technical lead. Active opportunity associations bring the correct people onto deal teams within their coverage, while accepted source and booked credit remain intact.

Full lesson
What you’ll be able to do

Maintain account context without confusing segmentation, coverage and authority.

Account context tells the team who is involved; explicit permissions determine what each person can do.

How to do it

  1. Confirm the account

    Use the correct customer or partner account and check existing ownership and history.

  2. Set the tier

    Select Tier 1, Tier 2 or Tier 3 using the agreed criteria; ask Sales Ops or RevOps to resolve unclear assignments.

  3. Maintain partner coverage

    Partner Ops updates the commercial and technical owners plus Partner Admin Contact; validate active users and the contact’s account. Confirm geographic scope and reconcile active deal teams when coverage changes; Account Tier alone does not determine access.

  4. Use the right notes

    Sales keeps customer/deal evidence in its normal fields. RevOps maintains restricted RevOps Notes; access follows the permissions matrix.

Questions to use

“Which agreed criteria support this tier?”

“Who owns commercial and technical coverage for this partner?”

What good looks like

Too vague or unsupported

Set Tier 1 because the opportunity is large; give the admin contact approval access.

Specific and supported

Tier follows agreed account criteria; active coverage owners and the partner admin contact verified; no approval entitlement inferred.

Put it into practice

A new Partner Admin Contact requests authority to approve pricing. Does adding that contact grant it?

Compare your answer

No. Partner Admin Contact is a relationship. Pricing approval follows the existing authority matrix and explicit entitlements.

Knowledge check

Can an assigned partner overlay change forecast or approve a registration simply because it is on the opportunity team?

Show answer and why

No. Team membership provides scoped collaboration only; those actions require separate authority.

Use this on your next deal

Account context tells the team who is involved; explicit permissions determine what each person can do.

02Work the dealBuild customer evidence, align the buying team and demonstrate why DDN is the preferred solution.+

Course overview

Learn how to turn initial interest into customer-supported evidence for DDN. Connect pain and decision criteria to the technical evaluation, build access to the buying team, and coordinate partners and customer actions while making risks visible.

What you’ll learn

  • Develop pain, technical and operational value, a champion, economic-buyer access and a decision path.
  • Run a purposeful POC and distinguish technical fit from customer-confirmed Technical Win.
  • Maintain meeting outcomes, owned follow-ups, partner plans, product requests and an accurate view of progress.

What you’ll take away

A supported deal position: the customer’s criteria, preferred solution, decision owners, unresolved blockers and dated next actions.

When to use it

Use this stream during discovery follow-up, solution design, buyer alignment and weekly deal reviews.

Identify Pain

The customer’s problem and its validated impact on people, operations or business results. It develops the initial CHAMPS Challenge into a clear reason to act.

At a glance

  • Follow the problem through to its consequence.
  • Validate who is affected and how often.
  • Complete before Stage 3 alongside the technical-fit evidence.

Scenario

Illustrative

The lab’s overnight staging delay sounds inconvenient. The AE learns it causes missed processing windows and repeat scheduling work. The customer confirms the impact. The opportunity now explains why the problem matters rather than relying on a generic performance claim.

Full lesson
What you’ll be able to do

Develop the initial challenge into a validated consequence that explains why the customer should act.

Keep the earlier CHAMPS Challenge. Identify Pain deepens it; Business Value later quantifies the economic case.

How to do it

  1. Revisit Challenge

    Begin with the original customer problem so the customer does not have to restart discovery.

  2. Follow the effects

    Ask what the disruption prevents, how frequently it occurs and who bears the consequence.

  3. Validate significance

    Ask the customer to correct your summary and distinguish proven impact from your hypothesis.

  4. Record the pain

    Save the evidence in Identify Pain and use it to guide technical evaluation and the later value case.

Questions to use

“What happens downstream when this delay occurs?”

“If the problem continues, what outcome is at risk?”

What good looks like

Too vague or unsupported

Customer unhappy with incumbent.

Specific and supported

Lab manager confirms staging delays cause missed processing windows and extra rescheduling each week.

Put it into practice

The customer says “performance is slow.” You do not yet know the business impact. Draft two follow-ups and a responsible provisional pain note.

Compare your answer

Ask: “What cannot happen while you wait?” and “How often does that affect the team?” Note: “Customer reports slow performance; downstream impact and frequency are not yet confirmed.” Develop the note only when the customer supplies evidence; do not invent savings.

Knowledge check

The customer confirms repeated rescheduling but cannot quantify its cost. Is the pain unusable until a dollar amount is available?

Show answer and why

No. Record the validated operational consequence and its frequency. Quantify the economic effect later with the customer. Preserve the original CHAMPS Challenge separately.

Use this on your next deal

Problem → who is affected → consequence → frequency → customer evidence. Keep unconfirmed financial impact explicit.

Understand competitors and the status quo

The alternatives the customer is considering, including other vendors and keeping the current approach.

At a glance

  • Ask which alternatives are actually being evaluated.
  • Include the do-nothing or incumbent alternative in the conversation.
  • When Other is selected, provide the company name.

Scenario

Illustrative

A customer compares DDN with two vendors but says keeping the existing platform is also possible. The AE asks what would make each path acceptable and records the verified competitors, keeping incumbent use distinct from active evaluation.

Full lesson
What you’ll be able to do

Separate active alternatives, incumbent use and the actual competitive outcome.

Incumbent vendors are what the customer uses today; Competitor Lost To records the outcome at loss. Those may differ.

How to do it

  1. Invite comparison

    Ask which approaches the customer is considering and why each remains credible.

  2. Test the status quo

    Explore what would cause the customer to defer the project or stay with the incumbent.

  3. Connect to criteria
